# Venue presets

Read at Stage 1, before the intake interview.

Stage 1 normally asks nine questions. Most talks fall into a small number of recurring types,
each with a stable answer to most of them. This file lets Stage 1 collapse to a single
confirmation line: state the assumed preset, then ask only what is genuinely still open for
*this* talk — the topic, the source material, anything topic-specific.

**How to use it:**

1. Infer the closest preset from the request — an explicit venue name, or context clues
   ("for the fellows", "for rounds", "for the conference").
2. State the assumption inline: *"I'm assuming this is [preset] — [duration], [audience],
   [tone], [disclosures]. Correct? And tell me: [topic] plus [remaining open question]."*
3. If confirmed or not objected to, go straight to Stage 2. If nothing matches, run the full
   nine-question intake.
4. If a pattern recurs twice and is not captured here, propose adding it as a preset. Keep this
   to a handful — a long tail of presets is worse than none.

> **Edit these before your first deck.** Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own
> institutions and typical slots. The preset *shapes* are the reusable part; the specifics are
> not. Five minutes here saves the intake interview every time after.

## Grand rounds / bedside teaching — [your department]

- **Audience:** attendings, residents, and fellows — mixed seniority, clinically sharp,
  time-pressured.
- **Duration:** 15–20 min, leaving room for discussion.
- **Tone:** authoritative but conversational. Peer-to-peer among clinicians, not a stage keynote.
- **Disclosures:** not required for internal departmental teaching, unless the topic touches
  industry-sponsored data.
- **Rigor:** high — clinical decisions may follow directly from this talk. Full evidence
  mapping, guideline cross-check where relevant.
- **References:** AMA, on-screen citation for any practice-changing claim.
- **Notes pace:** 120 wpm.

## Academic lecture — [your university]

- **Audience:** students or residents in a teaching context. Less assumed background than
  rounds; the deck should also stand alone as study material.
- **Duration:** 45–60 min for a lecture block; 20–30 min for a seminar slot.
- **Tone:** didactic. Build from first principles before the nuance. More on-screen structure
  is acceptable here — a learning-objectives slide is fine in this preset, unlike the others.
- **Disclosures:** standard academic disclosure slide if any funding or COI exists.
- **Rigor:** high, but the pace allows more foundational slides before the evidence-heavy core.
- **References:** AMA.
- **Notes pace:** 110–120 wpm — slower than rounds, to allow teaching pauses.

## Scientific congress — [ESC / ACC / AHA / your society]

- **Audience:** subspecialists. Highly expert, skeptical by default, comparing this talk to the
  best in the field in real time.
- **Duration:** strict and externally imposed — typically 8–12 min plus Q&A. **Confirm the exact
  slot; never assume.**
- **Tone:** maximum data rigor, minimal narrative softening. Congress register — data readout,
  not narrative beats, except possibly the hook.
- **Disclosures:** **mandatory.** Disclosure slide immediately after the title, per most
  congress requirements. Always confirm funding and COI at intake; never assume none.
- **Rigor:** maximum. Two-source cross-check on every central claim; flag anything preliminary
  explicitly.
- **References:** AMA, every claim cited on screen.
- **Notes pace:** 130 wpm — congress delivery runs tighter than teaching.
- **Language:** confirm explicitly. International congresses default to English even when the
  abstract was drafted in another language.

## Journal club

- **Audience:** department peers and fellows, in a critical-appraisal mindset. The deck must
  surface methodology and limitations, not just headline results.
- **Duration:** 15–20 min plus open critique.
- **Tone:** analytical. Include a limitations/critique slide — in this format that is expected
  and valued, not a weakness to hide.
- **Disclosures:** not required.
- **Rigor:** high on methodological precision — study design, statistical methods, risk of bias.
  Lower on breadth, since it is usually one paper rather than a topic survey.
- **References:** AMA — the paper under discussion plus two or three supporting or contrasting
  studies.
- **Notes pace:** 120 wpm.

## Internal / quick update

- **Audience:** varies — administrative, planning, or informal.
- **Duration:** 5–10 min.
- **Tone:** direct, low design overhead.
- **Disclosures:** not required.
- **Rigor:** lighter pass — user-supplied material plus a confirming search. Reserve deep
  cross-checking for the one or two central claims.
- **References:** light. Full citation only where a specific number is quoted.
- **Notes pace:** 120 wpm.

---

## Defaults that hold regardless of preset

- **Style:** Evidence & Air, unless the venue mandates its own branding. Ask about branding only
  at congress tier.
- **Research standard:** Standing Rule 1 applies to every preset without exception, *including*
  the quick-update tier. Only the **depth** of cross-checking scales down — never the currency
  or credibility bar.
- **Quality bar:** Standing Rule 2 applies to every preset. Informal stakes change pacing and
  content depth, never production quality.
